Afterword - What the hell did I just read?

Oh. That? A retelling of the Crisis of '24?

To preface this, I genuinely did not intend for my first actual blog to be about doomerisms like this. I wanted to post about a cat. But something happened. And I thus wanted to post about this instead.

However, let pre-9-pm me tell you all about it while I am still in control.

(If the 9 pm thing doesn't make sense, here's a nugget of wisdom from the internet:

"Never trust yourself after 9 pm and before 9 am."
Got me through many-a mental crisis.)

But enough of that: I sound like an absolutely self-centered, ego-inflated idiot, don't I?

Maybe I do.

Maybe I am.

(Author's note: no no this is enough. We are in prose, not poetry.)

During those one-and-a-half years, I was doing way more than study and excel. I literally put myself into every single opportunity I had been given, and to be honest, while I derived joy out of just doing those things, e.g., as mentioned above, helping out with clubs, student action stuff, managing and running projects, helping teachers make presentations, I could honestly confess that I did expect something, some recognition in return for my work. (See how selfish I am now?)

(Wait. I did just kind of leak my age, didn't I?)

And when that didn't come, I can only say, "the higher you fly, the worse your fall becomes".

I saw other people around me - whom I (to put it not so humbly) objectively totally surpassed academically and in extracirriculars - getting all the recognition, all the awards, all the love from teachers for doing exactly what I have been doing, getting the results I have been all along, and I lost it. Especially because those people were too my bullies.

Of course, as mentioned, it wasn't that gradual drip of disappointment that hit me the worst. Oh no. In the span of one day, I saw an award for going 'above and beyond', one which I had my eyes on for very long, go to my bully, and then proceeded to be told that a role, after a 'full score' (yes the teacher actually told me that) interview, was to be given to another person because 'they wouldn't get another opportunity in life ever again'.

I felt beyond frustrated. I did cry that day.

And yes. I did go to my teacher, the same one who was responsible for both disappointments (I guess she wasn't really 'responsible', per se), and voiced my disappointment. Yes. I was told that 'I already have the satisfaction from my good grades', and that 'I should ignore my bullies because I ultimately would get the last laugh anyways'.

But dear reader. Step into my shoes. I worked way more and reaped no reward. I saw my bullies be given what I thought I would get. And I was told that I shouldn't be disappointed, or even expect a reward at the first place.

Yes I sound like a selfish idiot. Of course one would rejoice more when a Prodigal Son returns than when the Elder Brother stays and works, yet would you not cry? Would you not lament about an injustice done?

But I continued. Very stupid. I know. I continued running errands, doing all that I could outside of studies to prove that I was, in fact, 'excellent'.

I think you can piece the rest of the story together from the poem itself. Disappointment never left it grew with each progressing incident. Neither did the relentless annoyance of my bullies. At a point it felt like nobody even cared that I was doing all this for them or for others.

I got into a BadTM place mentally (well maybe not as bad as many other people out there. I really shouldn't be whining about this at the first place if I 'have all this', but EH) for quite some time, until public exam prep started I withdrew myself from working at such a capacity, and my bullies too got too busy with preparations.

While I do feel better now, the terrible, crushing feelings still come back to haunt me often, especially when I see bullies flaunt their illegitemately earnt rewards in front of me. It still hurts. I still question why I work at all, yesterday being a major example. But sure. The fact that I can talk about this at this capacity today says just how much I have improved.
What's the takeaway, then?
Not much, really. Just know that I sometimes may write stuff that sounds quite doomy because of how I felt that day, and that, oh yeah, if feel like your hard work isn't going anywhere, and other people seem to be winning by cheating, don't stop. There are people out there who feel like you, without people like you, brilliance no longer blooms in the background, and if you stop working, it would only be a detriment to yourself. They keep winning with lies regardless.

Give yourself a pat on the back, and remind yourself that you are actually brilliant.

See you next week on the Dialogues (if I manage to chase the schedule)!
