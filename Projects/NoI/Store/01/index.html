And there you go, you get the cosine and sine magnitudes (mξr and mξi) for the input frequency ξ back (with a known multiplier which can be factored out) as a complex number, meaning this formulation of the FT works+.
'How is this useful?'
By knowing how to break any sound (which is essentially the g(t) I messed around with) down back into known functions, we essentially get the ability to 'make more of the sound' algorithmically. This means that we can speed up videos without making people sound super high pitched (by pitching them back down), or we can make VOCALOID (and UTAU^) music. Or even, you can make autotune from this.

But then again. This formulation of the FT isn't the one computers use (because a sound sample is neither continuous nor infinite, and computers do trigonometry too slowly). Search the Fast Fourier Transform up if you want to figure that one out.
